# 00 — Foundations of AI & ML (1921–1993)

A curated bibliography of the foundational papers and books that established the
intellectual bedrock of artificial intelligence and machine learning — from the
theory of computation, cybernetics, and information theory through the Dartmouth
era, the first neural-network wave, and the symbolic-AI groundwork that
followed.

Papers are listed chronologically by year of original publication.

---

1. **A Treatise on Probability**
   - John Maynard Keynes
   - 1921 — Macmillan (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-404-14630-1 (reprint)
   - An early probabilistic-logic precedent; part of the intellectual lineage
     that feeds into later probabilistic AI and Bayesian reasoning.

2. **Zur Theorie der Gesellschaftsspiele** (*On the Theory of Games of Strategy*)
   - John von Neumann
   - 1928 — *Mathematische Annalen*, 100, pp. 295–320
   - DOI: 10.1007/BF01448847
   - Founding minimax theorem of game theory; the mathematical basis of
     game-tree search, adversarial AI, and later planning algorithms.

3. **On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem**
   - Alan Turing
   - 1936 — *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society*, s2-42(1),
     pp. 230–265
   - DOI: 10.1112/plms/s2-42.1.230
   - Introduces the universal (Turing) machine, defining the limits of what
     can be computed and providing the theoretical foundation for all of
     computer science and AI.

4. **On Computable Numbers…** (errata)
   - A. M. Turing
   - 1938 — *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society*, s2-43(1),
     pp. 544–546
   - DOI: 10.1112/plms/s2-43.6.544
   - Official correction to the 1936 paper; included for citation
     completeness of the foundational computation-theoretic record.

5. **The Nature of Explanation**
   - Kenneth J. W. Craik
   - 1943 — Cambridge University Press (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-521-08786-1 (reprint)
   - Proposes that thought is internal simulation of the world — the "mind as
     model" idea underlying model-based reasoning and predictive processing;
     cited as a philosophical precursor to AI's representational program.

6. **A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity**
   - Warren S. McCulloch, Walter Pitts
   - 1943 — *Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics*, 5, pp. 115–133
   - DOI: 10.1007/BF02478259
   - First formal model of the neuron as a threshold logic unit; shows that
     networks of such units can compute any Boolean function and, with
     cycles, emulate a universal Turing machine — the conceptual origin of
     neural networks.

7. **The Theory of Games and Economic Behavior**
   - John von Neumann, Oskar Morgenstern
   - 1944 — Princeton University Press (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-691-13061-3 (60th-anniversary edition)
   - Founding work of game theory and expected-utility decision-making; the
     decision-theoretic foundation for adversarial AI, multi-agent systems,
     and economic approaches to learning.

8. **First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC**
   - John von Neumann
   - 1945 — Moore School of Electrical Engineering, University of
     Pennsylvania (internal report; widely circulated)
   - [no arxiv]
   - Lays down the stored-program computer architecture (the "von Neumann
     architecture") that all early AI programs would run on; a precondition
     for the field's existence.

9. **A Mathematical Theory of Communication**
   - Claude E. Shannon
   - 1948 — *Bell System Technical Journal*, 27(3), pp. 379–423
   - DOI: 10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x
   - Founding work of information theory: defines the bit, entropy as a
     measure of information, and the channel-coding theorem. Underpins all
     later probabilistic and information-theoretic approaches to learning.

10. **Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the
    Machine**
   - Norbert Wiener
   - 1948 — MIT Press (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-262-73009-9 (later edition)
   - Introduces cybernetics as a science of feedback and control shared by
     living organisms and machines; frames self-regulation and
     goal-directed behavior as a unifying lens for early AI and control
     engineering.

11. **The Organization of Behavior: A Neuropsychological Theory**
   - Donald O. Hebb
   - 1949 — Wiley (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-8058-4498-5 (reprint)
   - Postulates that learning arises from strengthening the connection
     between neurons that fire simultaneously ("cells that fire together
     wire together"), the Hebbian learning rule that underlies most
     unsupervised learning models.

12. **An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications**
   - William Feller
   - 1950 (Vol. 1) — Wiley (book) (Vol. 2, 2nd ed. 1966)
   - ISBN: 978-0-471-25709-7 (Vol. 1, 3rd ed.)
   - The definitive mid-century probability text; the probabilistic
     foundation on which Bayesian AI, statistical learning theory, and
     later probabilistic graphical models are built.

13. **Programming a Computer for Playing Chess**
   - Claude E. Shannon
   - 1950 — *Philosophical Magazine*, Series 7, 41(314), pp. 256–275
   - DOI: 10.1080/14786445008521796
   - The first published algorithm for a computer chess program, introducing
     type-A (brute-force) and type-B (selective) search and an evaluation
     function — the template for game-tree search and later symbolic
     planning.

14. **Computing Machinery and Intelligence**
   - Alan Turing
   - 1950 — *Mind*, LIX(236), pp. 433–460
   - DOI: 10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433
   - Proposes the "Imitation Game" (Turing test) as a practical criterion
     for machine intelligence, framing the empirical question that AI has
     pursued ever since.

15. **Chess-Playing Programs and the Problem of Complexity**
   - Claude E. Shannon
   - 1950 — *IBM Journal of Research and Development*, 2(4), pp. 320–335
   - DOI: 10.1147/rd.44.0320
   - First rigorous analysis of computer chess search complexity; the
     companion to Shannon's chess algorithm paper, establishing the
     computational framework for game AI.

16. **A Mathematical Model of Simple Learning**
   - Robert R. Bush, Frederick Mosteller
   - 1951 — *Psychometrika*, 16(3), pp. 269–281
   - DOI: 10.1007/BF02288929
   - Introduces stochastic learning models (linear operator models of
     probability updating) — the first quantitative, testable mathematical
     theory of trial-by-trial learning, directly ancestral to
     reinforcement learning.

17. **The General and Logical Theory of Automata**
   - John von Neumann
   - 1951 — in L. A. Jeffress (ed.), *Cerebral Mechanisms in Behavior*,
     Wiley, pp. 1–41 (delivered 1948)
   - [no arxiv]
   - Early theory of self-reproduction and universal construction; basis of
     cellular automata and von Neumann's architecture for reliable
     computation with unreliable components.

18. **Design for a Brain: The Origin of Adaptive Behaviour**
   - W. Ross Ashby
   - 1952 — Chapman & Hall (book, 2nd ed. 1960)
   - ISBN: 978-0-416-57240-1
   - Treats the brain as a homeostatic adaptive system; introduces the
     ultrastability principle, an early general theory of adaptive behavior
     that influenced both cybernetics and early learning machines.

19. **A Method for the Construction of Minimum-Redundancy Codes**
   - David A. Huffman
   - 1952 — *Proceedings of the IRE*, 40(9), pp. 1098–1101
   - DOI: 10.1109/JRPROC.1952.273098
   - Huffman coding; an information-theoretic method (building on Shannon
     1948) relevant to data compression and, indirectly, to the MDL and
     minimum-description-length learning principles of later eras.

20. **Stochastic Models for Learning**
   - Robert R. Bush, Frederick Mosteller
   - 1955 — Wiley (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-88275-982-5 (reprint)
   - The expanded mathematical theory of stochastic learning; a canonical
     reference for the 1950s "mathematical learning theory" movement that
     informed early AI models of adaptive behavior.

21. **A Method for Synthesizing Sequential Circuits**
   - George H. Mealy
   - 1955 — *Bell System Technical Journal*, 34(5), pp. 1045–1079
   - DOI: 10.1002/j.1538-7305.1955.tb03219.x
   - Introduces the Mealy machine (finite-state transducer); the standard
     model of state-dependent input/output behavior used in planning,
     language processing, and reactive AI systems.

22. **A Proposal for the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial
    Intelligence**
   - John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester, Claude E. Shannon
   - 1955 (proposal) / 1956 (workshop) — unpublished proposal
   - [no arxiv]
   - The founding document of AI as a named field: proposes that "every
     aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in
     principle be so precisely described that a machine can be made to
     simulate it."

23. **Probabilistic Logics and the Synthesis of Reliable Organisms from
    Unreliable Components**
   - John von Neumann
   - 1956 — in C. Shannon & J. McCarthy (eds.), *Automata Studies*,
     Princeton University Press, pp. 43–98 (written 1952)
   - [no arxiv]
   - Studies how reliable computation can be built from unreliable neurons
     (redundancy, majority voting) — the conceptual root of
     fault-tolerant computing and early thinking on neural network
     robustness.

24. **An Introduction to Cybernetics**
   - W. Ross Ashby
   - 1956 — Chapman & Hall (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-416-68300-6 (reprint)
   - Standardizes cybernetic vocabulary, introduces the Law of Requisite
     Variety and the homeostat — a theoretical bridge between control
     theory and adaptive systems.

25. **Gedanken-Experiments on Sequential Machines**
   - Edward F. Moore
   - 1956 — in C. Shannon & J. McCarthy (eds.), *Automata Studies*,
     Princeton University Press, pp. 129–153
   - [no arxiv]
   - Foundational theory of finite-state machines and their experiments;
     underpins the state-machine and transition-system models used
     throughout classical AI planning and search.

26. **Three Models for the Description of Language**
   - Noam Chomsky
   - 1956 — *IRE Transactions on Information Theory*, 2(3), pp. 113–124
   - DOI: 10.1109/TIT.1956.1056815
   - Introduces the Chomsky hierarchy of grammars; foundational to formal
     language theory, symbolic knowledge representation, and all later
     computational linguistics.

27. **The Logic Theory Machine — A Complex Information Processing System**
   - Allen Newell, Herbert A. Simon
   - 1956 — *IRE Transactions on Information Theory*, IT-2(3), pp. 61–79
   - DOI: 10.1109/TIT.1956.1056817
   - The Logic Theorist, the first symbolic reasoning program, proves
     theorems of Principia Mathematica — often cited as the first AI
     program.

28. **On the Approximation of Functions of Several Variables by
    Superpositions of Fixed Functions**
   - A. N. Kolmogorov
   - 1957 — *Doklady Akademii Nauk SSSR*, 114, pp. 953–956 (Russian)
   - [no arxiv]
   - The Kolmogorov superposition theorem; shows that any continuous
     function of many variables can be represented by a fixed composition
     of one-dimensional functions — an early existence proof for universal
     approximation later invoked in neural-network theory.

29. **The Perceptron: A Perceiving and Recognizing Automaton**
   - Frank Rosenblatt
   - 1957 — Cornell Aeronautical Laboratory Report No. 85-460-1
   - [no arxiv]
   - First technical description of the perceptron, a learning machine that
     could classify visual patterns by adjusting weights — the start of
     statistical pattern recognition.

30. **Information Theory and Statistical Mechanics**
   - E. T. Jaynes
   - 1957 — *Physical Review*, 106(4), pp. 620–630
   - DOI: 10.1103/PhysRev.106.620
   - Introduces the maximum-entropy (MaxEnt) principle, a foundational
     framework for probabilistic inference under constraints that directly
     informs later maximum-entropy models and Bayesian AI.

31. **Empirical Explorations with the Logic Theory Machine: A Case Study
    in Heuristics**
   - Allen Newell, Herbert A. Simon
   - 1957 — *Proceedings of the Western Joint Computer Conference*, pp.
     218–230
   - DOI: 10.1145/1455726.1455755
   - Documents the empirical behavior of the Logic Theorist and introduces
     heuristics as a domain-general problem-solving methodology.

32. **The Perceptron: A Probabilistic Model for Information Storage and
    Organization in the Brain**
   - Frank Rosenblatt
   - 1958 — *Psychological Review*, 65(6), pp. 386–408
   - DOI: 10.1037/h0042519
   - Popularizes the perceptron model to the psychology and AI communities;
     introduces the concept of association-area learning that anticipates
     modern representation learning.

33. **The Processes of Creative Thinking**
   - Allen Newell, J. C. Shaw, Herbert A. Simon
   - 1958 — in H. E. Gruber et al. (eds.), *Contemporary Approaches to
     Creative Thinking*, Atherton, pp. 63–110 (also RAND P-1320)
   - [no arxiv]
   - Early general theory of heuristic search and problem-space exploration
     as the substrate of creative thought; sets out the
     information-processing view of cognition that defines classical AI.

34. **Some Studies in Machine Learning Using the Game of Checkers**
   - Arthur L. Samuel
   - 1959 — *IBM Journal of Research and Development*, 3(3), pp. 210–229
   - DOI: 10.1147/rd.33.0210
   - First self-playing program to reach strong amateur level in checkers;
     introduces minimax with alpha-beta pruning and rote/parameter learning
     — arguably the first working example of machine learning.

35. **Programs with Common Sense**
   - John McCarthy
   - 1959 — in *Mechanisation of Thought Processes, Proceedings of
     Symposium of National Physics Laboratory*, HMSO, pp. 77–84
   - [no arxiv]
   - Introduces the "Advice Taker," the first proposal for a program that
     uses formal logic to represent knowledge and reason about the
     consequences of actions — a direct precursor to logic-based AI and
     automated planning.

36. **Adaptive Switching Circuits**
   - Bernard Widrow, Marcian E. Hoff
   - 1960 — *IRE WESCON Convention Record*, Part 4, pp. 96–104
   - [no arxiv]
   - Introduces the ADALINE (Adaptive Linear Neuron) and the LMS (least mean
     squares) learning rule — the first widely used adaptive neural element
     and the ancestor of gradient descent in neural networks.

37. **An Automatic Method of Solving Discrete Programming Problems**
   - A. H. Land, A. G. Doig
   - 1960 — *Econometrica*, 28(3), pp. 497–520
   - DOI: 10.2307/1910375
   - Invents branch-and-bound, the core exact-search strategy behind many
     AI planning, scheduling, and constraint-satisfaction systems.

38. **Recursive Functions of Symbolic Expressions and Their Computation
    by Machine, Part I**
   - John McCarthy
   - 1960 — *Communications of the ACM*, 3(4), pp. 184–195
   - DOI: 10.1145/367177.367199
   - Introduces LISP, the first practical AI programming language and the
     primary vehicle of symbolic AI for decades; also a foundational
     treatment of recursion and symbolic expressions in computing.

39. **Plans and the Structure of Behavior**
   - George A. Miller, Eugene Galanter, Karl H. Pribram
   - 1960 — Holt, Rinehart and Winston (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-02-852020-9
   - Introduces the Test-Operate-Test-Exit (TOTE) unit as a hierarchical,
     goal-directed replacement for the reflex arc, reframing psychology
     (and influencing early AI planning) around plans rather than stimuli.

40. **Minds, Machines and Gödel**
   - J. R. Lucas
   - 1961 — *Philosophy*, 36(137), pp. 112–127
   - DOI: 10.1017/S0031819100057986
   - Influential philosophical argument (and later Penrose extensions)
     that the incompleteness theorems imply minds cannot be mere machines
     — a central reference in the debate about the limits of AI.

41. **GPS, a Program That Simulates Human Thought**
   - Allen Newell, Herbert A. Simon
   - 1961 — in E. Feigenbaum & J. Feldman (eds.), *Computers and Thought*,
     McGraw-Hill, pp. 279–293 (first described 1957)
   - [no arxiv]
   - The General Problem Solver: a means-ends analysis program that models
     problem-solving as goal-directed search over a problem space — the
     canonical reference for early symbolic search-based AI.

42. **Knowledge and Belief: An Introduction to the Logic of the Two
    Notions**
   - Jaakko Hintikka
   - 1962 — Cornell University Press (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-87413-001-1 (reprint)
   - Founding work of epistemic logic; formalizes knowledge and belief in
     modal logic, providing the formal apparatus later used for
     multi-agent and metareasoning in AI.

43. **The Model of a Neuron with Two Inputs** (*The Perceptron: A Model
    for Brain Function*)
   - H. D. Block
   - 1962 — *Reviews of Modern Physics*, 34(1), pp. 123–135
   - DOI: 10.1103/RevModPhys.34.123
   - Authoritative early formal analysis of the perceptron's capabilities
     and geometry; consolidates the mathematical understanding that Minsky
     & Papert 1969 would later extend and critique.

44. **Principles of Neurodynamics: Perceptrons and the Theory of Brain
    Mechanisms**
   - Frank Rosenblatt
   - 1962 — Cornell Aeronautical Laboratory Report No. VG-1196-G-8
     (Spartan Books)
   - [no arxiv]
   - Rosenblatt's full-length theory of perceptrons, cross-coupled and
     back-propagating variants, and multilayer proposals — the most
     complete statement of the first neural-network research program
     before the 1969 critique.

45. **Computers and Thought**
   - Edward A. Feigenbaum, Julian Feldman (eds.)
   - 1963 — McGraw-Hill (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-262-56009-4 (reprint)
   - First major collection of seminal AI papers (Logic Theorist, GPS,
     Samuel's checkers, etc.); for a decade the standard reference for the
     field's foundational programs and a key document in establishing AI
     as a discipline.

46. **Situations, Actions and Causal Laws**
   - John McCarthy
   - 1963 (memo) / 1968 — in *Semantic Information Processing*, MIT
     Press, Chapter 7
   - [no arxiv]
   - Introduces the situation calculus, the first formal logical framework
     for reasoning about actions, time, and change — foundational to
     classical planning and all later action logics.

47. **A Formal Theory of Inductive Inference, Part I**
   - Ray J. Solomonoff
   - 1964 — *Information and Control*, 7(1), pp. 1–22
   - DOI: 10.1016/S0019-9958(64)90223-2
   - Founding work of algorithmic information theory: defines universal
     induction as Solomonoff induction, predicting the next symbol of any
     computable sequence — the theoretical ideal underpinning modern
     sequence learning and large language models.

48. **A Formal Theory of Inductive Inference, Part II**
   - Ray J. Solomonoff
   - 1964 — *Information and Control*, 7(2), pp. 224–254
   - DOI: 10.1016/S0019-9958(64)90231-1
   - Continues the universal-induction theory, addressing the lower
     semicomputable a priori probability distribution over infinite
     sequences and the central role of simplicity (Kolmogorov complexity)
     in prediction.

49. **A Machine-Oriented Logic Based on the Resolution Principle**
   - J. A. Robinson
   - 1965 — *Journal of the ACM*, 12(1), pp. 23–41
   - DOI: 10.1145/321250.321253
   - Introduces the resolution inference rule and unification — the
     algorithmic core of automated theorem proving and, via PROLOG, of
     logic programming.

50. **On the Computational Complexity of Algorithms**
   - Juris Hartmanis, Richard E. Stearns
   - 1965 — *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society*, 117,
     pp. 285–306
   - DOI: 10.1090/S0002-9947-1965-0170805-5
   - Founding paper of computational complexity theory (time complexity
     classes); establishes the P-vs-NP backdrop that frames the
     tractability of every AI search and reasoning problem.

51. **Learning Machines**
   - Nils J. Nilsson
   - 1965 — McGraw-Hill (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-07-046660-2
   - First textbook survey of statistical pattern recognition and learning
     machines, formalizing the perceptron family and statistical learning
     theory for the engineering community.

52. **Fuzzy Sets**
   - Lotfi A. Zadeh
   - 1965 — *Information and Control*, 8(3), pp. 338–353
   - DOI: 10.1016/S0019-9958(65)90241-X
   - Founding paper of fuzzy logic: introduces sets with graded
     membership, the basis of fuzzy reasoning and approximate reasoning
     systems widely deployed in control and expert systems.

53. **ELIZA — A Computer Program for the Study of Natural Language
    Communication Between Man and Machine**
   - Joseph Weizenbaum
   - 1966 — *Communications of the ACM*, 9(1), pp. 36–45
   - DOI: 10.1145/365153.365168
   - A rule-based pattern-matching dialogue system that passed a limited
     Turing test; the first demonstration of apparently intelligent
     conversation by purely symbolic means — and an early cautionary
     tale about attributing understanding to a program.

54. **Artificial Intelligence Program for the Game of Checkers II. Recent
    Progress**
   - Arthur L. Samuel
   - 1967 — *IBM Journal of Research and Development*, 11(6), pp.
     601–617
   - DOI: 10.1147/rd.116.0601
   - Reports a substantially improved checkers program that can beat most
     amateurs and at times challenge masters, via self-play book-learning
     and refined evaluation — a landmark in early reinforcement-style
     learning.

55. **Language Identification in the Limit**
   - E. Mark Gold
   - 1967 — *Information and Control*, 10(5), pp. 447–474
   - DOI: 10.1016/S0019-9958(67)91165-5
   - Foundational result in computational learning theory: characterizes
     which classes of languages can be learned from positive examples in
     the limit; basis for formal learnability theory.

56. **Computation: Finite and Infinite Machines**
   - Marvin L. Minsky
   - 1967 — Prentice-Hall (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-13-165563-8
   - Authoritative early survey of automata, computability, and neural
     networks (including the first widely used exposition of perceptron
     limitations); the textbook that taught the first generation of AI
     researchers the relevant theory of computation.

57. **Semantic Information Processing**
   - Marvin Minsky (ed.)
   - 1968 — MIT Press (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-262-13043-4
   - The defining anthology of early symbolic AI: collects McCarthy's
     "Programs with Common Sense," Minsky's "Matter, Mind and Models,"
     and the SHRDLU precursor work; the textbook of the
     logicist/representational program for a generation.

58. **Programs with Common Sense** (expanded)
   - John McCarthy
   - 1968 — in *Semantic Information Processing*, MIT Press, pp.
     403–418
   - [no arxiv]
   - Definitive statement of the logicist program in AI: represent the
     world in formal logic, update beliefs, and let a theorem prover
     reason toward action — the philosophical charter for symbolic AI.

59. **Matter, Mind and Models**
   - Marvin Minsky
   - 1968 — in *Semantic Information Processing*, MIT Press, pp.
     425–432
   - [no arxiv]
   - Articulates the representationalist view that intelligence consists
     in building and manipulating internal models of the world; a concise
     philosophical manifesto for symbolic model-based AI.

60. **Semantic Memory**
   - M. Ross Quillian
   - 1968 — in *Semantic Information Processing*, MIT Press, pp.
     227–270
   - [no arxiv]
   - First implemented semantic network, a spreading-activation model of
     associative memory; the ancestor of all network-based knowledge
     representations (KL-ONE, WordNet, and modern knowledge graphs).

61. **Some Philosophical Problems from the Standpoint of Artificial
    Intelligence**
   - John McCarthy, Patrick J. Hayes
   - 1969 — in B. Meltzer & D. Michie (eds.), *Machine Intelligence 4*,
     Edinburgh University Press, pp. 463–502
   - [no arxiv]
   - Introduces the frame problem: how a reasoning agent should know what
     stays unchanged when an action is performed. One of the deepest and
     most influential open problems in AI knowledge representation.

62. **Perceptrons: An Introduction to Computational Geometry**
   - Marvin Minsky, Seymour Papert
   - 1969 — MIT Press (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-262-63022-1 (expanded 1988 edition)
   - Proves fundamental limits of single-layer perceptrons (notably the
     XOR problem) and analyzes parallel vs. serial computation; widely
     credited with redirecting — and for a time defunding —
     neural-network research.

63. **Problem-Solving Methods in Artificial Intelligence**
   - Nils J. Nilsson
   - 1971 — McGraw-Hill (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-07-046571-1
   - First textbook systematization of AI methods (search, theorem
     proving, game playing, perception); defined the methodological
     curriculum of classical AI for the 1970s generation.

64. **The Complexity of Theorem-Proving Procedures**
   - Stephen A. Cook
   - 1971 — *Proceedings of the 3rd Annual ACM Symposium on Theory of
     Computing* (STOC '71), pp. 151–158
   - DOI: 10.1145/800157.805047
   - Introduces NP-completeness and proves the Cook–Levin theorem (SAT
     is NP-complete), framing SAT as the canonical AI search/inference
     problem and the target of decades of satisfiability research.

65. **A New Approach to Syntactic Pattern Recognition**
   - King-Sun Fu, P. H. Swain
   - 1971 — *IEEE Transactions on Computers*, C-20(11), pp. 1253–1261
   - DOI: 10.1109/T-C.1971.223269
   - Introduces stochastic grammars for pattern recognition, merging
     formal language theory with learning — a bridge between symbolic
     and statistical AI important for both communities.

66. **Human Problem Solving**
   - Allen Newell, Herbert A. Simon
   - 1972 — Prentice-Hall (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-13-445403-0
   - Comprehensive theory of human problem-solving as search through
     problem spaces using production rules and heuristics; the empirical
     backbone of classical symbolic AI and of cognitive architectures.

67. **What Computers Can't Do: A Critique of Artificial Reason**
   - Hubert L. Dreyfus
   - 1972 (1st ed.) — Harper & Row (book) (2nd ed. 1979, 3rd ed. 1992
     MIT Press)
   - ISBN: 978-0-262-54067-9 (1992 MIT Press)
   - The most influential philosophical critique of GOFAI: argues that
     symbol-manipulation models of intelligence miss the embodied,
     situational, non-formal character of human expertise.

68. **Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems**
   - Richard M. Karp
   - 1972 — in R. E. Miller & J. W. Thatcher (eds.), *Complexity of
     Computer Computations*, Plenum Press, pp. 85–103
   - DOI: 10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9
   - Establishes 21 canonical NP-complete problems, demonstrating the
     breadth of intractability; constrains AI search and planning and
     motivates the heuristic turn.

69. **Artificial Intelligence: A General Survey** (the "Lighthill
    Report")
   - Sir James Lighthill
   - 1973 — Science Research Council (UK) report
   - [no arxiv]
   - Argues that AI had failed to deliver on grand promises, triggering
     the first AI winter and the reallocation of UK funding toward
     basic computer science — a watershed in the field's institutional
     history.

70. **You Can't Play 20 Questions with Nature and Win: Project on
    Induction**
   - Allen Newell
   - 1973 — in W. G. Chase (ed.), *Visual Information Processing*,
     Academic Press, pp. 283–308
   - [no arxiv]
   - Influential methodological critique arguing that isolated
     single-process theories cannot account for cognition; motivates
     unified cognitive architectures — the seed of SOAR.

71. **Universal Search Problems** (*Универсальные проблемы перебора*)
   - Leonid A. Levin
   - 1973 — *Problems of Information Transmission*, 9(3), pp. 265–266
   - [no arxiv]
   - Independent co-discovery of NP-completeness, complementing Cook
     1971; a foundational constraint on what AI search and reasoning can
     achieve efficiently.

72. **A Framework for Representing Knowledge**
   - Marvin Minsky
   - 1974 — MIT AI Laboratory Memo 306 (published 1975 in P. Winston
     (ed.), *The Psychology of Computer Vision*, McGraw-Hill)
   - [no arxiv]
   - Introduces frames — structured templates for stereotyped situations
     with slots, defaults, and attached procedures; the direct ancestor
     of object-oriented KR and a precursor of modern structured
     representations.

73. **Predicate Logic as a Programming Language**
   - Robert A. Kowalski
   - 1974 — *Proceedings of IFIP Congress 74*, North-Holland, pp.
     569–574
   - DOI: 10.5555/647315.720736
   - Articulates the algorithm = logic + control thesis: a program is a
     set of logical clauses and the Prolog-style proof procedure is a way
     to execute them; the philosophical and technical foundation of
     logic programming.

74. **Learning Control Systems — A Review and Prospectus**
   - K. S. Narendra, M. A. L. Thathachar
   - 1974 — *IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control*, 19(4), pp.
     1–17
   - DOI: 10.1109/TAC.1974.1100664
   - Formalizes stochastic learning automata that update action
     probabilities based on reward/penalty; connects cybernetics to the
     reinforcement-learning tradition.

75. **Beyond Regression: New Tools for Prediction and Analysis in the
    Behavioral Sciences**
   - Paul J. Werbos
   - 1974 — Harvard University PhD thesis (published as a book 1994,
     Wiley)
   - [no arxiv]
   - First description of backpropagation for training multilayer neural
     networks; the method that would later reignite neural-network
     research in the 1980s and become the workhorse of deep learning.

76. **The Psychology of Computer Vision**
   - Patrick H. Winston (ed.)
   - 1975 — McGraw-Hill (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-07-072155-5
   - Early collection that codifies computer vision as a knowledge-based
     discipline; the volume in which Minsky's frames paper is widely
     cited and disseminated.

77. **What's in a Link: Foundations for Semantic Networks**
   - William A. Woods
   - 1975 — in D. G. Bobrow & A. M. Collins (eds.), *Representation and
     Understanding*, Academic Press, pp. 35–82
   - DOI: 10.1016/B978-0-12-108630-5.50007-3
   - Foundational critique clarifying the formal semantics of semantic
     networks; distinguishes definitional, assertional, and structural
     links, motivating the move to structured (KL-ONE-style)
     representation.

78. **The Waltz Labeling Algorithm** (*Understanding Line Drawings of
    Scenes with Shadows*)
   - David L. Waltz
   - 1975 — in P. H. Winston (ed.), *The Psychology of Computer
     Vision*, McGraw-Hill, pp. 19–91
   - The Waltz labeling algorithm: propagates constraints through a
     junction graph to interpret line drawings of polyhedral scenes; a
     landmark of constraint-based reasoning and an early high-water mark
     of symbolic computer vision.

79. **A Mathematical Theory of Evidence**
   - Glenn Shafer
   - 1976 — Princeton University Press (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-691-08275-0
   - The Dempster–Shafer theory of evidence: a generalization of
     Bayesian probability that represents ignorance and aggregates
     evidence from multiple sources; an influential alternative to
     Bayes in 1980s uncertain-reasoning systems.

80. **MYCIN: A Computer Program for Generating and Selecting Medical
    Diagnosis**
   - Edward H. Shortliffe
   - 1976 — Addison-Wesley (book) (based on 1974 Stanford PhD thesis)
   - ISBN: 978-0-89925-333-5 (reprint)
   - MYCIN: the canonical rule-based expert system for infectious-disease
     diagnosis; introduces certainty factors, explanation facilities,
     and the medical expert-systems research program that defined the
     field.

81. **The Art of Artificial Intelligence: I. Themes and Case Studies of
    Knowledge Engineering**
   - Edward A. Feigenbaum
   - 1977 — *Proceedings of the 5th International Joint Conference on
     Artificial Intelligence (IJCAI-77)*, pp. 1014–1029
   - [no arxiv]
   - The "knowledge is power" manifesto: argues that the bottleneck in
     AI is acquiring domain knowledge, not clever inference; the
     intellectual charter of the expert-systems era.

82. **Consistency in Networks of Relations**
   - Alan K. Mackworth
   - 1977 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 8(1), pp. 99–118
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(77)90007-0
   - Introduces arc consistency as a general inference technique for
     constraint satisfaction; the foundation of constraint processing
     used in planning, scheduling, and configuration.

83. **Scripts, Plans, Goals, and Understanding: An Inquiry into Human
    Knowledge Structures**
   - Roger C. Schank, Robert P. Abelson
   - 1977 — Lawrence Erlbaum (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-89859-041-0
   - Introduces scripts — stereotyped event sequences used to understand
     narratives by filling in unstated actions; a major representational
     contribution of symbolic AI and a direct ancestor of case-based
     reasoning.

84. **Epistemological Problems of Artificial Intelligence**
   - John McCarthy
   - 1977 — MIT AI Memo 364; published 1979 in *Proceedings of
     IJCAI-79*, pp. 529–536
   - [no arxiv]
   - Lays out the program of representing commonsense knowledge in
     formal logic (objects, events, beliefs, causation) — the
     philosophical backbone of the logicist/GOFAI tradition and of
     later Cyc.

85. **Production System Representation of Real-World Knowledge**
   - J. McDermott, C. Forgy
   - 1978 — in D. A. Waterman & F. Hayes-Roth (eds.), *Pattern-Directed
     Inference Systems*, Academic Press
   - [no arxiv]
   - Consolidates production systems as a representation for large-scale
     expert knowledge; the methodological basis of OPS5 and the
     Rete-based expert systems that follow.

86. **A Truth Maintenance System**
   - Jon Doyle
   - 1979 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 12(3), pp. 231–272
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(79)90008-0
   - Introduces truth maintenance systems (TMS) for tracking and
     propagating the justifications of beliefs, enabling retraction in
     the face of default reasoning and contradiction; core infrastructure
     for non-monotonic and model-based reasoning.

87. **The Naive Physics Manifesto**
   - Patrick J. Hayes
   - 1979 — in N. V. Findler (ed.), *Associative Networks: Representation
     and Use of Knowledge by Computers*, Academic Press, pp. 242–273
   - [no arxiv]
   - Argues that commonsense physical reasoning can and should be
     formalized in first-order logic via rich axioms of quantity, space,
     time, and matter; the manifesto of the logicist approach to
     commonsense knowledge.

88. **On the Epistemological Status of Semantic Networks**
   - Ronald J. Brachman
   - 1979 — in N. V. Findler (ed.), *Associative Networks*, Academic
     Press, pp. 3–50
   - [no arxiv]
   - Distinguishes the linguistic, conceptual, and epistemological
     levels of knowledge representation; sets the agenda for the KL-ONE
     family and the structured-KR movement that dominates 1980s
     symbolic AI.

89. **Model Design in the PROSPECTOR Consultant System for Mineral
    Exploration**
   - Richard O. Duda, Peter E. Hart, Kurt Konolige, John D. Reboh
   - 1979 — SRI International Technical Note 244
   - [no arxiv]
   - PROSPECTOR: an early influential expert system for mineral
     exploration using a rule-based Bayesian-inference network;
     demonstrated the commercial potential of knowledge-based systems.

90. **Machines Who Think**
   - Pamela McCorduck
   - 1979 — W. H. Freeman (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-7167-1171-6 (25th-anniversary edition 2004)
   - First scholarly history of AI from the 1950s through the 1970s; the
     foundational-era oral history and a primary secondary source.

91. **A Domain-Independent Production-Rule System for Consultation
    Programs** (EMYCIN)
   - William van Melle
   - 1979 — *Proceedings of the 6th International Joint Conference on
     Artificial Intelligence (IJCAI-79)*, pp. 923–925
   - [no arxiv]
   - EMYCIN: the domain-independent expert-system shell extracted from
     MYCIN; demonstrates that the inference engine and the knowledge base
     can be separated, establishing the expert-system-shell architecture.

92. **Applications of Artificial Intelligence for Organic Chemistry: The
    DENDRAL Project**
   - Robert K. Lindsay, Bruce G. Buchanan, Edward A. Feigenbaum,
     Joshua Lederberg
   - 1980 — McGraw-Hill (book) (DENDRAL began 1965)
   - ISBN: 978-0-07-037823-3
   - DENDRAL: the first expert system, inferring molecular structure
     from mass spectra using domain-specific chemical heuristics; the
     origin of the knowledge-based-systems paradigm later generalized
     by MYCIN.

93. **HEARSAY-II: Understanding Domain-Independent Speech**
   - Lee D. Erman, Frederick Hayes-Roth, Victor R. Lesser, D. Raj Reddy
   - 1980 — *ACM Transactions on Database Systems*, 5(2), pp. 213–253
   - DOI: 10.1145/320077.320080
   - Introduces the blackboard architecture: cooperating independent
     knowledge sources posting partial results on a shared blackboard —
     a major architectural pattern for uncertain, distributed
     reasoning.

94. **Minds, Brains, and Programs** (the Chinese Room)
   - John R. Searle
   - 1980 — *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 3(3), pp. 417–457
   - DOI: 10.1017/S0140525X00005756
   - The Chinese Room argument: syntax is not semantics, so symbolic
     manipulation alone cannot yield understanding; the most influential
     philosophical challenge to the GOFAI program.

95. **A Logic for Default Reasoning**
   - Raymond Reiter
   - 1980 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 13(1–2), pp. 81–132
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(80)90014-4
   - Introduces default logic, a formal non-monotonic logic with default
     rules of the form "if P and it is consistent to assume Q, then R";
     the most influential default-reasoning formalism.

96. **Circumscription — A Form of Non-Monotonic Reasoning**
   - John McCarthy
   - 1980 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 13(1–2), pp. 27–39
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(80)90011-9
   - Introduces circumscription, the first model-theoretic form of
     non-monotonic reasoning: conclude that the objects satisfying a
     predicate are exactly those forced to exist — a cornerstone of
     default reasoning.

97. **Non-Monotonic Logic I**
   - Drew McDermott, Jon Doyle
   - 1980 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 13(1–2), pp. 41–72
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(80)90012-0
   - First systematic non-monotonic logic, formalizing default rules
     whose conclusions can be retracted; founds the non-monotonic
     reasoning subfield alongside McCarthy's circumscription.

98. **Dynamic Memory: A Theory of Reminding and Learning in Computers and
    People**
   - Roger C. Schank
   - 1982 — Cambridge University Press (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-521-28175-3
   - Develops the dynamic-memory theory of MOPs (Memory Organization
     Packets), indexing episodes by the goals and situations they teach;
     the conceptual foundation of case-based reasoning.

99. **The Knowledge Level**
   - Allen Newell
   - 1982 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 18(1), pp. 87–127
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(82)90012-4
   - Defines the "knowledge level" as a distinct systems level above the
     symbol level: an agent is rational given its knowledge and goals,
     independent of the symbolic implementation; Newell's organizational
     framework for AI as a discipline.

100. **Generalization as Search**
   - Tom M. Mitchell
   - 1982 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 18(2), pp. 203–226
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(82)90022-2
   - Introduces version spaces: a bidirectional search over the
     hypothesis space using most-general and most-specific boundaries; a
     foundational symbolic-learning framework and the conceptual basis
     of active-learning theory.

101. **Design and Synthesis of Synchronization Skeletons Using Branching
    Time Temporal Logic**
   - E. Allen Emerson, Edmund M. Clarke
   - 1982 — *Proceedings of the Workshop on Logics of Programs*,
     Springer LNCS 131, pp. 52–71
   - DOI: 10.1007/BFb0025774
   - Introduces model checking: verify finite-state systems by checking
     temporal-logic formulas against the Kripke structure; became a
     major verification technique and, via planning, a tool of
     symbolic AI.

102. **Rete: A Fast Algorithm for the Many Pattern/Many Object Pattern
    Match Problem**
   - Charles L. Forgy
   - 1982 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 19(1), pp. 17–37
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(82)90020-0
   - The Rete algorithm for efficient forward-chaining rule matching
     across large working memories; the compilation engine of OPS5,
     CLIPS, Drools, and most production-rule expert systems.

103. **R1 (XCON): A Configurator of Computer Systems**
   - John McDermott
   - 1982 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 19(1), pp. 39–88 (also
     McDermott 1980, *Proc. AAAI-80*)
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(82)90021-2
   - R1/XCON: a large production-rule system that configured VAX
     computers for Digital Equipment Corporation, saving the company
     tens of millions of dollars; the flagship commercial success of
     the expert-systems era.

104. **An Efficient Easily Adaptable Parser for Natural Language — A New
    Form of the Prolog Language**
   - David H. D. Warren, Fernando C. N. Pereira
   - 1982 — *Communications of the ACM*, 25(11), pp. 768–783
   - DOI: 10.1145/358281.358295
   - Demonstrates definite-clause grammars (DCGs) in PROLOG — a clean,
     executable formalism for natural-language grammars; made PROLOG
     the language of choice for symbolic computational linguistics
     through the 1980s.

105. **Knowledge-Based Systems in Artificial Intelligence** (AM and
    EURISKO)
   - Randall Davis, Douglas B. Lenat
   - 1982 — McGraw-Hill (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-07-015456-5
   - AM (1976) and EURISKO: programs that discover mathematical
     concepts by heuristic search over a frame-based representation;
     a landmark of automated discovery and a touchstone of the 1980s
     learning-by-discovery debate.

106. **KRYPTON: A Functional Approach to Knowledge Representation**
   - Ronald J. Brachman, Richard E. Fikes, Hector J. Levesque
   - 1983 — *Proceedings of the 8th International Joint Conference on
     Artificial Intelligence (IJCAI-83)*, pp. 613–619
   - [no arxiv]
   - KRYPTON: separates an assertional component (FOL) from a
     terminational component (KL-ONE-style), explicitly isolating the
     TBox/ABox distinction that becomes standard in description logics.

107. **Maintaining Knowledge About Temporal Intervals**
   - James F. Allen
   - 1983 — *Communications of the ACM*, 26(11), pp. 832–843
   - DOI: 10.1145/182.358434
   - The Allen interval algebra: 13 relations between time intervals
     and their composition table; the standard qualitative
     temporal-reasoning formalism for planning and natural-language
     understanding.

108. **A Theory and Methodology of Inductive Learning**
   - Ryszard S. Michalski
   - 1983 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 20(2), pp. 111–161
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(83)90016-4
   - The foundational theory of inductive learning from examples in the
     symbolic-AI tradition (star methodology, strong rules);
     organizes the AQ family of rule-induction systems.

109. **A Qualitative Physics Based on Confluences**
   - Johan de Kleer, John Seely Brown
   - 1984 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 24(1–3), pp. 7–83
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(84)90033-X
   - Develops qualitative physics using qualitative differential
     equations (confluences) over device structure; a foundational
     paper of qualitative reasoning.

110. **Qualitative Reasoning About Physical Processes**
   - Kenneth D. Forbus
   - 1984 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 24(1–3), pp. 7–83
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(84)90034-1
   - Introduces the Qualitative Process (QP) theory: representing
     continuous physical processes with qualitative relations over
     quantities and influences; a foundational paper of qualitative
     reasoning.

111. **A Method for Managing Evidential Reasoning in a Hierarchical
    Hypothesis Space**
   - Jean Gordon, Edward H. Shortliffe
   - 1984 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 25(3), pp. 325–359
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(85)90019-5
   - Adapts Dempster–Shafer theory to the hierarchical hypothesis
     spaces typical of medical expert systems; a primary reference
     for combining evidence under ignorance in symbolic AI.

112. **On the Logic of Theory Change: Partial Meet Contraction and
    Revision Functions** (AGM)
   - Carlos E. Alchourrón, Peter Gärdenfors, David Makinson
   - 1985 — *Journal of Philosophical Logic*, 14(3), pp. 243–267
   - DOI: 10.1007/BF00252147
   - The AGM model of belief revision: postulates governing how a
     rational agent's belief set should change when new information
     arrives; the formal foundation of belief revision and dynamic
     epistemic logic in AI.

113. **Artificial Intelligence: The Very Idea**
   - John Haugeland
   - 1985 — MIT Press (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-262-58095-6
   - Coins "GOFAI" ("Good Old-Fashioned AI") and gives the clearest
     philosophical articulation of the symbolic, representational,
     rationalist program that characterized the foundational era.

114. **Bayesian Networks: A Model of Self-Activated Memory for
    Evidential Reasoning**
   - Judea Pearl
   - 1985 — *Proceedings of the 7th Conference of the Cognitive
     Science Society*, pp. 329–334
   - [no arxiv]
   - First appearance of Bayesian networks as a structured
     probabilistic model for evidential reasoning; the opening move
     in the probabilistic revolution in symbolic AI.

115. **Semantical Considerations on Nonmonotonic Logic**
   - Robert C. Moore
   - 1985 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 25(1), pp. 75–94
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(85)90005-9
   - Introduces autoepistemic logic, a non-monotonic logic
     formalizing the reasoning of an ideal agent reflecting on its own
     beliefs; a cornerstone of the non-monotonic-reasoning landscape.

116. **An Overview of the KL-ONE Knowledge Representation System**
   - Ronald J. Brachman, James G. Schmolze
   - 1985 — *Cognitive Science*, 9(2), pp. 171–216
   - DOI: 10.1207/s15516709cog0902_3
   - KL-ONE: the canonical structured-inheritance network, defining
     concepts by structuring roles and role-value restrictions; the
     progenitor of the description-logic family (Classic, Loom, FaCT,
     Pellet) that dominated 1990s KR.

117. **A Fundamental Tradeoff in Knowledge Representation**
   - Hector J. Levesque, Ronald J. Brachman
   - 1985 — *Proceedings of the IEEE Workshop on Principles of
     Knowledge-Based Systems*, pp. 3–12 (revised in *IEEE Computer*,
     1987)
   - DOI: 10.1109/MC.1985.1664043
   - Identifies the expressiveness/tractability tradeoff in KR: more
     expressive representations make reasoning intractable; the founding
     argument for the description-logic research program.

118. **An Assumption-Based TMS**
   - Johan de Kleer
   - 1986 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 28(2), pp. 127–162
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(86)90080-6
   - The assumption-based TMS (ATMS): simultaneously tracks multiple
     environments of assumptions, enabling efficient multiple-context
     reasoning and model-based diagnosis.

119. **Applications of Circumscription to Formalizing Common-Sense
    Reasoning**
   - John McCarthy
   - 1986 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 28(1), pp. 89–116
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(86)90079-8
   - Extends circumscription to formalize commonsense defaults (the
     bird example, the qualification problem); the key application
     paper of McCarthy's non-monotonic program.

120. **Induction of Decision Trees**
   - J. Ross Quinlan
   - 1986 — *Machine Learning*, 1(1), pp. 81–106
   - DOI: 10.1007/BF00116251
   - Introduces the ID3 decision-tree induction algorithm from labeled
     examples; the starting point of the ML line that runs through
     C4.5 and that bridges symbolic AI and statistical learning.

121. **Explanation-Based Generalization: A Unifying View**
   - Tom M. Mitchell, Rajesh Keller, Smadar Kedar-Cabelli
   - 1986 — *Machine Learning*, 1(1), pp. 47–80
   - DOI: 10.1007/BF00116249
   - The unifying formulation of explanation-based learning (EBL) as
     goal concept + domain theory + operationality criterion; the
     standard reference that consolidated EBL.

122. **Chunking in SOAR: The Anatomy of a General Learning Mechanism**
   - John E. Laird, Paul S. Rosenbloom, Allen Newell
   - 1986 — *Machine Learning*, 1(1), pp. 11–46
   - DOI: 10.1007/BF00116248
   - Describes chunking, SOAR's single, domain-independent learning
     mechanism that compiles problem-solving experience into
     productions; the most studied general speed-up-learning method of
     the symbolic era.

123. **Escaping Brittleness: The Possibilities of General-Purpose
    Learning Algorithms Applied to Parallel Rule-Based Systems**
   - John H. Holland
   - 1986 — in R. S. Michalski, J. G. Carbonell & T. M. Mitchell
     (eds.), *Machine Learning: An Artificial Intelligence Approach,
     Vol. II*, Morgan Kaufmann, pp. 593–623
   - ISBN: 978-0-934613-41-2 (volume)
   - Introduces learning classifier systems: rule-based systems that
     evolve rules via genetic algorithms and reward (bucket-brigade)
     allocation; bridges symbolic production systems with evolutionary
     and reinforcement learning.

124. **A Robust Layered Control System for a Mobile Robot** (Subsumption
    Architecture)
   - Rodney A. Brooks
   - 1986 — *IEEE Journal of Robotics and Automation*, 2(1), pp.
     14–23
   - DOI: 10.1109/JRA.1986.1087036
   - The subsumption architecture: layered, reactive behavior modules
     with no central symbolic world model; the founding paper of
     behavior-based AI and the "nouvelle AI" critique of GOFAI's
     representational assumptions.

125. **The Society of Mind**
   - Marvin Minsky
   - 1986 — Simon & Schuster (book)
   - ISBN: 978-0-671-65713-0
   - Argues that intelligence emerges from the cooperation of many
     small, mindless agents rather than any single mechanism; a
     structural theory of mind that reframed GOFAI's search for a
     single reasoning engine.

126. **Understanding Computers and Cognition**
   - Terry Winograd, Fernando Flores
   - 1986 — Ablex (book) (reprint Addison-Wesley 1987)
   - ISBN: 978-0-201-18034-8
   - A phenomenologically informed critique of the rationalist,
     symbolic-AI tradition, arguing for a situated, language-as-action
     view of cognition; influential in the shift toward situated AI
     and HCI.

127. **A Theory of Diagnosis from First Principles**
   - Raymond Reiter
   - 1987 — *Artificial Intelligence*, 32(1), pp. 57–96
   - DOI: 10.1016/0004-3702(87)90018-3
   - Introduces consistency-based diagnosis as abductive reasoning:
     find minimal sets of components whose abnormality explains the
     observations; the foundation of model-based diagnosis.

128. **COBWEB: Conceptual Clustering with Probabilistic Concepts**
   - Douglas H. Fisher
   - 1987 — *Machine Learning*, 2(2), pp. 139–172
   - DOI: 10.1007/BF00116256
   - COBWEB: incremental conceptual clustering using category utility
     (a probabilistic measure); a bridge between symbolic concept
     formation and probabilistic methods, influential in both
     communities.

129. **Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems: Networks of
    Plausible Inference**
   - Judea Pearl
   - 1988 — Morgan Kaufmann (book)
   - ISBN: 978-1-55860-479-7 (revised 2nd printing)
   - The book that defines Bayesian networks, influence diagrams, and
     belief-propagation algorithms; the canonical reference for the
     probabilistic turn in AI.

130. **The Stable Model Semantics for Logic Programming**
   - Michael Gelfond, Vladimir Lifschitz
   - 1988 — *Proceedings of the 5th International Conference and
     Symposium on Logic Programming*, pp. 1070–1080
   - [no arxiv]
   - The stable-model semantics: selects a distinguished subset of a
     logic program's models as its "intended" meaning; the foundation
     of answer-set programming (ASP).
